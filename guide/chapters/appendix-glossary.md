# Glossary

**Altitude.** How high something is above the horizon, in degrees: 0° on
the horizon, 90° straight overhead.

**Arcminute (′), arcsecond (″).** Small angles: 60 arcminutes make a
degree, and 60 arcseconds an arcminute. The full Moon is about 31′ across.

**Astronomical dusk and dawn.** When the Sun is 18° below the horizon, in
the evening and the morning. Between them is astronomical darkness, when
the sky is as dark as it gets.

**Azimuth.** The compass direction of something, in degrees: 0° north, 90°
east, 180° south, 270° west.

**Best Month.** The month when a target is best placed for imaging from a
location. See [Best Month Calculation](appendix-best-months.md).

**Bortle scale.** A rating of sky darkness from 1, the darkest skies, to
9, an inner city.

**Circumpolar.** Never setting: always above the horizon, or above your
minimum altitude, from your location.

**Current Target.** The target the Daily Visibility, Yearly Observability,
and Viewfinder views show. See [Introduction](introduction.md).

**Declination (Dec).** A target's position north or south of the
celestial equator, in degrees, like latitude on Earth.

**Dither.** A small move of the mount between exposures, so that hot
pixels and noise land in different places on each sub and stack out.

**DSS.** The Digitized Sky Survey, the photographic survey of the whole
sky that the Viewfinder's sky images come from.

**Field of view.** How much sky your camera records through your
telescope, in degrees.

**Flip Offset, Flip Pause.** How long after the target crosses the
meridian the meridian flip starts, and how long before it imaging stops.
See [Sequence Planner](sequence-planner.md).

**Guide RMS.** How far, on average, the guide star wandered from where the
mount was trying to hold it, in arcseconds. Lower is better.

**Horizon profile.** The height of the trees, buildings, and hills around
a location, in each direction. See [Getting Started](getting-started.md).

**Integration time.** The total exposure time of the subs you stack.

**J2000.** The standard reference for sky coordinates, fixed at the year
2000. The target database uses it.

**Magnitude.** A measure of brightness: the larger the number, the
fainter the object.

**Meridian.** The line across the sky from north to south through the
point overhead. A target is at its highest when it crosses it.

**Meridian flip.** On an equatorial mount, the turn of the telescope to
the other side of the mount after a target crosses the meridian, so it can
keep tracking.

**Minimum altitude.** The lowest altitude you're willing to image at. It's
**Min Altitude** in Settings, and can be changed in several views.

**Narrowband.** Imaging through filters that pass only the light of a few
gases, such as hydrogen-alpha. It cuts through moonlight and light
pollution.

**Pinned Targets.** Your shortlist for the next night, listed in the
sidebar. The Sequence Planner plans them.

**Plate solving.** Working out exactly where a telescope is pointing by
matching the stars in an image against a catalog.

**Right ascension (RA).** A target's east–west position on the sky, in
hours, minutes, and seconds, like longitude on Earth.

**Seeing.** How much the air blurs stars, usually in arcseconds. Good
seeing is 2″ or less.

**Sub.** A single exposure, one of the many that are stacked into an
image.

**Sub gap.** The time between the end of one sub and the start of the
next, while the camera saves the image.

**Transit.** A target crossing the meridian, when it's at its highest.

**Transparency.** How clear the air is: how much haze, smoke, or thin
cloud dims the stars.
